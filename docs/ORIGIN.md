# Ternux — The Story Behind My Mobile Computing Journey

*A personal account of a Mobile-Only Workflow, continuous learning, research, and building an independent computing environment.*

## 1. A Different Way of Working

One consistent part of my professional and technical life has been managing my complete digital-work workflow through mobile devices. This was not a temporary workaround. I organized the entire process around mobile: planning, creating, editing, communicating, documenting, and delivering work.

My professional journey began in 2015 on Fiverr, where I worked in Email Marketing, Web Design, and Graphic Design. From the beginning, I handled the complete workflow for these jobs through mobile devices. I used Android applications, online services, and different digital tools to complete each task.

My familiarity with computers, operating systems, Linux, and software-related technologies predates that professional beginning. The mobile-centered approach did not emerge because I discovered these technologies later. It developed alongside my existing technical familiarity and practical experience, as I consistently chose mobile devices as the environment for my professional digital workflow.

## 2. Mobile-Only Workflow Across Professional Work

This approach continued during my professional experience in Saudi Arabia. While working as a Progress Reporter at PCMC, I completed reporting, documentation, and office tasks on mobile using WPS Office and other Office Suite applications.

Later, while working at the University of Jeddah, I needed to perform calculations and other work-related tasks. I handled those tasks through mobile applications as well.

After returning to Bangladesh, I worked as a Computer Operator, where the practical uses of my mobile workflow expanded further. Using OTG, I connected an Android phone directly to a printer to print photographs and documents. I also completed other digital office tasks using Android software and applications.

Today, in my work at Rabeya Education Family, I continue the same approach. I manage the complete workflow for documentation, office tasks, graphic design, and other digital activities from mobile. WPS Office, Picsart, and a range of Android applications are part of my everyday working environment.

The workplaces and responsibilities have changed, as have the applications and tools. What has remained consistent is the use of a Mobile-Only Workflow for my professional digital work.

## 3. Reading, Learning, Research, and Experimentation

Mobile is not only where I carry out professional work. For years, it has also been my everyday environment for studying, reading books, researching technical subjects, collecting information, maintaining notes, reading documentation, and learning new things.

Exploring a technology, studying its documentation, comparing approaches, running small tests, and checking whether an idea works in practice are all part of this mobile-centered routine.

Because I was already familiar with computers, Linux, and related technologies, this exploration was not my first introduction to the wider world of computing. Instead, I became increasingly interested in how familiar technologies and concepts could be used in a mobile environment, what limitations existed, and how those limitations could be addressed.

## 4. Expanding Computing on Android

Over time, my interest moved beyond conventional Android applications. I began experimenting with Termux, Linux-based environments, command-line tools, and desktop environments.

I then explored ways to bring Linux environments, desktop applications, development tools, graphics, and automation together on mobile hardware. The goal was to build a practical environment capable of supporting a wider range of computing tasks directly from an Android device.

At this stage, mobile was not only the device I used to do my work. It was also becoming the platform on which I could build, configure, test, and manage a computing environment of my own.

## 5. Programming and the AI Chapter

Exploring programming and artificial intelligence brought new momentum to this journey. AI-assisted development and AI agents opened up new possibilities for writing and analyzing code, conducting research, solving problems, and building software.

This made it increasingly practical to work on different software projects from mobile, experiment with new technologies, create custom tools, and tackle more complex technical challenges.

My long-standing Mobile-Only Workflow expanded further. The work was no longer limited to using mobile applications; it now included building and managing software, development environments, and computing systems directly through mobile devices.

## 6. ADT: From Mobile Computing to an Android Development Toolchain

The same practical needs led to another important project: [ADT (ARM64 Android Development Toolchain)](https://github.com/soobujmiah/adt). While working toward a capable Linux ARM64 environment centered on mobile devices, I needed a practical way to build, sign, install, inspect, and debug Android applications from that environment. Much of the conventional Android development tooling relies on x86_64 host binaries, making a complete development workflow difficult to run natively in ARM64 Linux—especially on an Android phone using Termux and PRoot Debian.

ADT grew out of that practical requirement. It is an Android development toolchain for Linux ARM64, bringing Android SDK build-tools, platform-tools, ADB, and related build and signing utilities into a usable ARM64 environment. Its purpose is not limited to Ternux; it aims to make Android development workflows practical on suitable Linux ARM64 systems, including Android phones.

Ternux and ADT are therefore related but distinct outcomes of the same Mobile Computing journey. Ternux provides a Linux desktop and broader computing environment on mobile devices; ADT provides the Android application development toolchain that can operate within that ARM64 Linux environment.

## 7. The Birth of Ternux

Ternux is a result of this long experience and continuous effort. It is more than a way to run Linux on Android. It is a project to build a practical, extensible, and integrated computing environment centered around mobile devices.

Through Ternux, I am working to bring together Linux environments, desktop experiences, development tools, automation, software management, and other technical capabilities in a cohesive system.

The goal is not simply to run separate applications. It is to make technologies work together, create workflows suited to real tasks, and make broader use of the capabilities of mobile devices.

Every experiment, problem solved, improvement, and practical use contributes to Ternux. It did not emerge from a single moment or an isolated idea. Its foundations grew out of years of working, learning, research, and technical exploration.

## 8. An Ongoing Journey

I continue to use mobile devices for my professional digital work, studying, reading, research, documentation, programming, and developing software projects. The journey moves forward through learning new technologies, experimenting, solving problems, and building new things.

Ternux is not a final destination. It is an ongoing effort to make my long-standing Mobile-Only Workflow more capable, organized, and technically rich.

**Ternux is not where the journey began. It is what years of working, learning, researching, and experimenting have gradually become.**
